# Diy Cricket Grip Machine 

A custom cricket bat gripping machine that uses a standard air pump and a 3D-printed body to make installing a grip easy. Measuring approximately 27 cm, it fits all adult cricket bats and some smaller sizes as well. It also features two one-way valves that build and maintain air pressure, ensuring you never have to worry about pressure loss.

## Overview

Many cricket players spend a frustratingly long time trying to change their bat grips. Worse yet, the immense force required can actually cause painful friction burns on your hands. To solve this problem, I designed a portable, 3D-printable solution that uses basic air pressure. By combining a simple air pump with two one-way check valves, this compact device lets you easily grip a bat anywhere. It fits right into your cricket bag just like a traditional gripping cone, but without any of the strain.

### What it does

Here is how the streamlined system works:

* **Pneumatic Vacuum Source:** Uses a standalone vacuum pump connected directly to the chamber to evacuate air without complex motor linkages.
* **Dual Check-Valve Manifold:** A pair of one-way check valves controls airflow direction. When the pump draws a vacuum, the inlet valve opens to pull air out of the chamber while the exhaust valve seals shut. When the pump cycles/vents, the inlet valve seals tight while the exhaust valve lets trapped air escape safely.
* **Soda-Can Chamber:** A ~6 cm wide chamber provides ideal clearance for standard cricket bat handles, allowing the rubber grip to fully expand against the inner walls.
* **Instant Clamping:** In just seconds, the setup hits negative pressure (-6 to -10 PSI) and holds the grip flat against the walls. Sliding the handle inside and releasing the valve clamps the grip perfectly onto the wood.

## Getting Started

1. **Download the CAD Files:** Download the 3D models from the [CAD folder](https://github.com/Shashvat1333/Diy-Cricket-Grip-Machine/tree/main/CAD). [View Onshape Model](https://cad.onshape.com/documents/6bd545eb6b8de829bdead483/w/cdbd6ac044726cc7e3085e07/e/91f4c85f78cc1a84885944af?renderMode=0&uiState=6aeff87bc55e9899ea6e2543)
2. **Print the Parts:** Import the STL files into your preferred slicer (such as PrusaSlicer) and send them to your 3D printer.
3. **Assemble the Chamber:** Join the printed components using super glue or hot glue, ensuring all seams and fitting joints are completely airtight.
4. **Test & Verify:** Power up the system and verify that the design pulls the grip flat against the inner tube walls. If it fails to hold suction, inspect all pneumatic connections and valve joints for leaks, then apply additional sealant as needed.
